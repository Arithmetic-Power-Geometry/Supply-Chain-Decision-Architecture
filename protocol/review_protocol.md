# Publication-Grade Review Protocol

## Objective
Evaluate whether a decision-centered, multi-dimensional architecture provides a reproducible and analytically useful synthesis of supply-chain-management research beyond existing function-, method-, technology-, and domain-centered taxonomies.

## Research questions
RQ1. How has the intellectual structure of SCM evolved across management, operations research, information systems, industrial engineering, and computer science?
RQ2. Which dimensions are required to distinguish SCM decision problems without conflating problem, method, technology, and evidence?
RQ3. How does SCDA compare with prior SCM classification frameworks in coverage, collision, distinguishability, and gap discovery?
RQ4. Which SCDA dimensions contribute non-redundant information?
RQ5. How mature is the evidence supporting major SCM methods and technologies across decisions and industries?
RQ6. Which sparse or contradictory regions of the architecture define high-value future research?

## Corpus design
Use two linked corpora:
1. Review-of-reviews corpus: major SCM reviews, taxonomies, frameworks, and surveys.
2. Primary-study corpus: stratified studies spanning historical eras, disciplines, methods, industries, and technologies.

## Retrieval
Search multiple scholarly databases using documented queries. Record database, query, date, result count, deduplication, and screening decisions. Supplement database retrieval with backward and forward citation chaining for seminal work.

## Inclusion
Peer-reviewed research materially addressing supply-chain management, supply networks, or an SCM decision/process; sufficient information for at least the decision-core dimensions; English-language scholarly publication; publication metadata verifiable.

## Exclusion
Pure logistics/production papers with no supply-chain relevance; editorials without analytical contribution; inaccessible metadata; duplicates; non-scholarly promotional material.

## Coding
Code each included study independently against the SCDA schema. Preserve source text/evidence for each nontrivial code. Record uncertainty rather than forcing a category.

## Reliability
A blinded second coding pass or independent second coder should be used on a substantial random sample. Report Cohen's kappa for categorical dimensions and raw agreement. Revise ambiguous codebook definitions before final corpus freeze.

## Baselines
Compare SCDA with dimensions instantiated from genuine prior frameworks identified in the review-of-reviews corpus. Simplified function-only, method-only, and technology-only schemes remain diagnostic controls, not literature baselines.

## Evaluation
- coding coverage
- collision rate
- distinguishability
- normalized entropy/information content
- pairwise redundancy between dimensions
- ablation by removing each dimension
- sparse-cell/gap discovery
- temporal stability
- inter-rater reliability
- sensitivity to alternative coding rules

## Evidence maturity
Keep evidence maturity separate from technological sophistication. Analyze whether advanced methods are supported by synthetic, benchmark, observational, pilot, deployed, or longitudinal evidence.

## Freeze rule
No headline empirical claim is written before:
1. corpus is frozen,
2. screening log is complete,
3. coding reliability is reported,
4. baseline definitions are fixed,
5. workflow reproduces all reported tables/figures,
6. robustness and ablation analyses pass.

## Reporting
Use PRISMA-style flow reporting where applicable, a review-of-reviews gap table, taxonomy provenance table, temporal evolution map, architecture heatmaps, baseline comparison, ablation results, evidence-maturity map, and a structured research agenda.
