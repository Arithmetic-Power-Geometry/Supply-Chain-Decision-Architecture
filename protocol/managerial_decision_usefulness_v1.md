# Managerial Decision-Usefulness Protocol v1

## Scope
This analysis tests whether SCDA-coded representations expose distinctions relevant to managerial interpretation. It does not claim that using SCDA improves profit, service level, resilience, adoption success or any other organizational outcome.

## Primary questions
1. Do claims sharing the same enabling technology correspond to different decision kernels?
2. Do claims sharing the same method correspond to different decision kernels?
3. Do claims sharing the same solution layer correspond to different decision kernels?
4. Among matched decision/solution claims, does evidence maturity distinguish research readiness?
5. Among matched decision/solution/evidence claims, does context distinguish where evidence was obtained?

## Decision kernel
Managerial decision identity is represented by decision level, process, flow and objective. Technology and method are not treated as substitutes for the decision itself.

## Diagnostics
For each shallow representation, group claims by its signature and measure the proportion belonging to groups that contain more than one decision-kernel signature. Report claim counts, ambiguity rates and independent-study counts.

Evidence maturity and context are interpreted conservatively. Evidence maturity may inform how directly a finding has been tested; it is not a deployment recommendation. Context identifies the environment represented in the evidence; it does not establish transferability to another organization.

## Case illustrations
Any managerial illustration in the paper must be traceable to coded corpus records. Synthetic examples may explain notation but cannot be presented as empirical evidence of usefulness.

## Claim boundary
Permitted conclusion: the representation reduces descriptive ambiguity or exposes evidence/context distinctions in the reviewed literature.
Not permitted without separate outcome data: SCDA improves managerial performance, produces better decisions, saves money, increases resilience, or causes superior adoption outcomes.

## Failure rule
If technology-only, method-only or solution-only descriptions are not materially more ambiguous than the candidate architecture on the real corpus, managerial-usefulness claims must be narrowed accordingly.
