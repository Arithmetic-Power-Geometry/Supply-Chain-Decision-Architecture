# Claim, Source and Reference Integrity Protocol v1

## Three distinct checks
1. Bibliographic identity: does the DOI/reference identify the intended publication?
2. Provenance completeness: is each analytical claim linked to a study and source locator?
3. Semantic support: does the cited source actually support the claim made?

These checks must never be collapsed. Metadata agreement cannot prove semantic support.

## Claim provenance ledger
Every headline empirical or literature-derived claim receives a stable claim_id, study_id, DOI where available, and source locator. Source locator should be sufficiently precise for re-audit: page, section, table, figure, result, abstract passage or other stable location.

Support status is one of:
- verified_support;
- partial_support;
- not_supported;
- pending_review.

Only a source-level human audit may move a claim out of pending_review. The automated validator checks completeness and consistency only.

## Bibliography audit
Before submission:
- every manuscript citation key must resolve to the bibliography;
- duplicate BibTeX keys are prohibited;
- duplicate DOI entries are prohibited unless documented as distinct versions;
- uncited bibliography entries are reviewed and normally removed;
- DOI metadata are checked against independent bibliographic metadata and, where disputed, the publisher/version of record.

## Claim correction rule
A claim marked partial_support must be narrowed to what the source establishes or supported with additional evidence. A claim marked not_supported cannot remain as written.

## Paper/artifact boundary
Repository artifacts provide provenance and reproducibility. They do not become evidence for literature claims merely because they were generated by the workflow.

## Freeze
The final claim-provenance ledger and reference audit are frozen before the submission PDF is generated. Any later substantive claim or citation change requires rerunning the integrity audit.
