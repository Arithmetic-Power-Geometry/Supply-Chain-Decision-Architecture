# Claim-Level Coding Rule

The unit of analysis for architecture testing is the focal research claim, not automatically the entire paper.

A paper may generate multiple claim records when substantively different claims rely on different decision identities, methods, technologies, or evidence forms.

Rules:
- Split only substantively distinct claims; do not multiply records for rhetorical restatements.
- Preserve paper ID so within-paper dependence remains visible.
- Do not treat claim records as independent papers in prevalence statistics.
- Blank theory means no explicit theory is defensibly assigned.
- Evidence is coded for the focal claim.
- Confidence is certain only when the source explicitly supports the coding; interpretive SCDA mappings remain probable.
- Multi-label extensions may be used in the final corpus, but the primary signature analysis requires a declared focal label plus preserved secondary labels.

Pilot consequence:
The first nested comparison may be run as a calibration diagnostic. It is not a manuscript result until independent reliability and the systematic corpus are available.
